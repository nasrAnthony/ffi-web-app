What is disability insurance?

Disability insurance replaces a portion of your income with a monthly benefit if you’re unable to work due to a covered illness or injury. Benefits help pay everyday bills—rent or mortgage, groceries, loans, and childcare—so you can focus on getting better.

How does it work?

After an elimination period (e.g., 30/60/90/120/180 days), monthly payments begin and continue until you recover or reach the chosen benefit period (e.g., 2 years, 5 years, or to age 65/70). Your ability to claim depends on the policy’s definition of disability (e.g., own-occupation or any-occupation). Optional riders can add partial/residual benefits, cost-of-living increases, and future purchase options.

Who is it best suited for?

Anyone who relies on their income—employees, contractors, professionals, and business owners. It’s especially helpful if you have fixed obligations (mortgage, family expenses, business overhead) or limited emergency savings. It can also complement employer long-term disability (LTD) plans.

Key features of a
disability insurance policy

Your income is your most valuable asset. Here’s how disability coverage can keep money coming in if health issues keep you from working.

01 Monthly Income Benefit

Replace a portion of your earnings

Receive a monthly benefit—often up to about 60–70% of gross income (subject to financial underwriting)—if you meet the policy’s disability definition.

02 Elimination & Benefit Periods

Timing that fits your savings and needs

Choose how long you self-fund before benefits begin (30–180+ days), and how long payments can last (e.g., 2 or 5 years, or to age 65/70). Longer elimination periods usually lower premiums.

03 Definition of Disability

Own-occupation vs. any-occupation

The claim standard matters. “Own-occupation” focuses on your ability to perform your regular job duties, while “any-occupation” is broader. We’ll help you compare definitions across carriers.

04 Partial & Residual Benefits

Support when you can work some but not all

Many policies pay proportionate benefits if you lose part of your income or hours. Return-to-work incentives and rehab support can help you transition back more smoothly.

05 Optional Riders

Customize protection for your career

Add Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA), Future Purchase Option, own-occupation enhancements, and more. Many individual policies are guaranteed renewable, and some are non-cancellable.

Frequently asked questions

How much coverage do I need?
A common guideline is up to ~60–70% of gross income, subject to financial underwriting and existing coverage. We’ll right-size the benefit to your budget and obligations.
I already have group LTD at work—do I still need this?
Group LTD is a great foundation, but it can be taxable if your employer pays the premium, may cap benefits, and often includes offsets (CPP/QPP, EI, WSIB, etc.). An individual policy can top up, add own-occupation protection, and stay with you if you change jobs.
What affects my price?
Age, health, smoking status, occupation class, income, benefit amount, elimination/benefit periods, and riders all influence premiums. We compare carriers to find strong value for your situation.
Are benefits taxable?
Generally, individually owned benefits are tax-free when premiums are paid with after-tax dollars. Employer-paid group LTD benefits are typically taxable. Always confirm with your advisor and tax professional.
