Frequently asked questions

Are disability benefits subject to taxes?
Yes, if your employer covers the premium. However, if you personally fund the policy, the benefits you receive are typically free from taxes.
Can this policy be used for estate planning?
Yes, this policy helps minimize estate taxes while facilitating a smooth transfer of wealth to your beneficiaries, ensuring your legacy is preserved.
What happens if I stop paying the premiums?
If premiums stop, the policy’s cash value can help cover the costs, but extended non-payment may result in a reduced death benefit, which could impact your coverage.
What occurs when the term of the policy ends?
At the policy’s end, you have the option to renew, convert it into permanent insurance, or simply let the coverage lapse.
Who should consider term life insurance?
Term life insurance is well-suited for those seeking simple, budget-friendly coverage, especially for purposes like replacing lost income or protecting a mortgage.
Do I need a medical exam?
Some policies require a medical exam, but there are also options available that don’t necessitate one.
Can I change my term life policy to permanent insurance?
Yes, many term life policies offer the option to convert to permanent coverage without requiring a medical exam.
Who would benefit from critical illness insurance?
Critical illness insurance is especially useful for those with a family history of severe health conditions or anyone seeking peace of mind against financial strain caused by serious illnesses.
Which illnesses are typically covered?
Most policies provide coverage for serious conditions such as heart attacks, strokes, cancer, and organ failure. For more detailed information, feel free to reach out to us directly.
Can I get coverage with a pre-existing condition?
This depends on the specific policy. Some may have waiting periods or exclusions for pre-existing conditions. For personalized guidance, contact us to discuss your concerns.
