What is partnership insurance?

Partnership insurance is coverage used to fund a buy-sell agreement between co-owners of a business. If a triggering event occurs—such as death or disability—the policy proceeds provide cash to purchase the departing owner’s shares, keeping control stable and protecting the company and the family.

How does it work?

First, owners set a written buy-sell agreement that defines triggers (death, disability, critical illness, retirement), valuation, and the purchase method. Then we arrange insurance to match that obligation—typically life insurance for death benefits and disability buy-out coverage for long-term disability. The policy proceeds flow according to the agreement so the transition is funded and fast.

Who is it best suited for?

Incorporated businesses and professional practices with two or more owners, especially where the company’s value is concentrated in the founders. It’s also valuable when successors are identified but lack the liquidity to buy shares without insurance funding.

Key components of a
buy-sell funding plan

Align the agreement, valuation, and funding so ownership transfers are timely, fair, and financed.

01 Buy-Sell Agreement

Define triggers, price, and process

Set out what happens at death, disability, critical illness, or retirement; who buys; and how price is determined. A clear agreement prevents disputes and delays.

02 Life Insurance Funding

Liquidity when it’s needed most

Use term or permanent life insurance sized to the buy-out obligation. Proceeds create immediate cash to purchase the deceased owner’s shares without straining company finances.

03 Disability Buy-Out

Plan for long-term incapacity

A disability buy-out policy funds the purchase if an owner can’t return to work after the waiting period. Options include lump-sum, instalment, or a combination.

04 Ownership Structure

Cross-purchase vs. corporate (entity)

Choose whether owners hold policies on each other (cross-purchase) or the corporation owns them (entity purchase). Each approach has pros and cons for admin, pricing, and tax—coordinate with legal/tax advisors.

05 Valuation & Reviews

Keep coverage aligned with value

Use a fixed value, formula (EBITDA/revenue multiple), or third-party appraisal—and review annually so insurance amounts track the business value.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need a buy-sell agreement before getting insurance?
It’s strongly recommended. Insurance should mirror the agreement’s triggers, valuation method, and purchase mechanics so funding and legal terms match at claim time.
How much coverage should we carry?
Start with the current equity value plus any expected adjustments (e.g., shareholder loans). We’ll help size coverage and set a review cadence so amounts stay current as the business grows.
What’s better: cross-purchase or corporate-owned?
Cross-purchase can be simpler for smaller partnerships and may align ownership step-ups with surviving owners. Corporate-owned centralizes premiums and proceeds. The right choice depends on ownership count, valuation, and tax—coordinate with your advisors.
Can we include critical illness coverage?
Yes—many partnerships add critical illness to provide liquidity on covered diagnoses. Your agreement should specify if and how a CI payout triggers a partial or full buy-out.
Any tax considerations?
Tax treatment varies by structure and province. We’ll work with your legal and tax advisors to align ownership, beneficiaries, and premium funding with current rules.
