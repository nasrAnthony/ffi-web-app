What is key person insurance?

Key person insurance is business-owned coverage on an essential individual—such as a founder, executive, top salesperson, lead engineer, or relationship owner. If that person dies or becomes disabled, the benefit is paid to the company to help offset lost profits, fund recruitment and training, reassure lenders and investors, and stabilize operations.

How does it work?

The corporation is the owner and beneficiary of a life insurance policy (term or permanent) and can also add key person disability coverage. You choose benefit amounts based on the person’s impact and the financial risks you want to cover (e.g., cash flow, loan covenants, replacement costs). Proceeds are paid to the company upon a covered event, subject to the policy terms.

Who is it best suited for?

Organizations of any size where knowledge, relationships, or production are concentrated in a few people. It’s especially useful for venture-backed companies, professional practices, firms with material client concentration, and businesses with lender or investor requirements tied to key personnel.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies as a “key person”?
Founders, partners, executives, lead engineers, top salespeople, principal practitioners, and anyone whose loss would materially disrupt revenue, operations, financing, or customer relationships.
How much coverage should we carry?
We typically model a blend of profit contribution (e.g., 1–3 years), replacement & ramp-up costs, and any lender/investor requirements. We’ll tailor amounts to your risk tolerance and budget.
Is key person insurance the same as buy-sell insurance?
No. Key person insurance protects the company against the loss of a pivotal individual. Buy-sell insurance funds a shareholder agreement to transfer ownership between partners. Many firms implement both.
Are premiums tax-deductible?
Tax treatment depends on benefit type and structure. Life insurance premiums are generally not deductible, while disability structures vary. Always confirm with your tax advisor for your jurisdiction.
Do we have to replace the person to claim?
No. Benefits are intended to help the company manage through the transition—funding recruitment, training, interim leadership, and sustaining operations while plans are executed.
