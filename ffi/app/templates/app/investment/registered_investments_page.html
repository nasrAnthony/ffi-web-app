RRSP — Registered Retirement Savings Plan

Designed for retirement. Contributions may reduce taxable income, and investments grow tax-deferred until withdrawal.

Why it’s useful

Spousal RRSPs can support income-splitting later. Programs like the Home Buyers’ Plan (HBP) and Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP) allow temporary withdrawals under specific rules.

Good fit

Higher earners today expecting a lower tax bracket in retirement; anyone building long-term retirement capital.

TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account

Flexible, powerful, and tax-free. Growth and withdrawals don’t create taxable income (rules apply).

Why it’s useful

Room accumulates annually. Withdrawals restore room the following year, making TFSA ideal for many goals and for managing taxable income in retirement.

Good fit

All savers. Especially helpful for variable income or when you expect to be in a higher bracket later.

FHSA — First Home Savings Account

Hybrid of RRSP and TFSA for first-time buyers: contributions may be deductible and qualifying withdrawals are tax-free for a first home.

Why it’s useful

Can be combined with the RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan. If unused, eligible transfers to RRSP may be possible without affecting RRSP room.

Good fit

Eligible first-time buyers who want to accelerate a down payment with dual tax advantages.

RESP — Registered Education Savings Plan

Education-focused saving with government grants (CESG). Growth is tax-deferred; students often withdraw at lower tax rates.

Why it’s useful

Family or individual plans, flexible beneficiaries, and structured educational assistance payments (EAP) when studies begin.

Good fit

Parents and grandparents funding future education while leveraging grants.

Frequently asked questions

Registered vs. non-registered — what’s the difference?
Registered accounts offer tax advantages (tax-deferred or tax-free); non-registered accounts do not but have fewer withdrawal rules.
Which account should I use first?
Common order: emergency fund → FHSA (if eligible) → TFSA/RRSP mix based on your bracket → RESP for kids. We’ll tailor it to you.
What can I invest in?
Diversified ETFs/funds, GIC ladders, and quality bonds are typical. We design to your horizon and risk capacity, then rebalance.
Are there withdrawal rules?
Yes—TFSAs are generally tax-free; RRSPs are taxable; RESP withdrawals follow EAP/ROE rules; FHSA has qualifying home rules. Ask us before moving funds.
