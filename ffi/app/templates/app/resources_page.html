Our FAQs

Whole-life Insurance FAQs

How does the cash value work in whole life insurance?
With each premium payment, your policy’s cash value grows. This value can be accessed during your lifetime, either through loans or withdrawals, offering a financial resource when needed.
Is whole life insurance more expensive than other types?
Generally, whole life insurance comes with higher premiums compared to term life insurance. However, it provides permanent coverage, with fixed premiums throughout the life of the policy, ensuring long-term financial security.
Can I access the cash value while I’m still alive?
Yes, you can access the cash value through loans or withdrawals. Be mindful that if the amount is not repaid, it could reduce both the death benefit and the cash surrender value.
Are the premiums fixed for whole life insurance?
Yes, the premiums are fixed for whole life insurance, meaning they remain the same throughout the duration of the policy, regardless of your age or health.

Universal-life Insurance FAQs

How does the investment component work?
The policy offers a selection of investment opportunities that allow your earnings to grow without being taxed until you withdraw them.
Can I withdraw money from my policy?
Yes, you have the option to withdraw from the policy's cash value. Keep in mind that doing so could reduce the amount of your death benefit.
Is this policy beneficial for estate planning?
Absolutely, this policy can help reduce your tax burden and allows for a seamless transfer of assets to your heirs.
What happens if I stop paying premiums?
If you cease premium payments, the accumulated cash value can be used to cover the costs. However, long-term non-payment may decrease the death benefit.

Term-life Insurance FAQs

What happens when the term expires?
At the end of the term, you can choose to renew the policy, convert it to permanent insurance, or let it lapse.
Who should consider term life insurance?
Term life insurance is ideal for those looking for straightforward and affordable coverage, often for purposes like replacing lost income or protecting a mortgage.
Is a medical exam necessary?
Some term policies do require a medical exam. However, there are also no-exam options available for certain policies.
Can I convert my term policy to permanent insurance?
Yes, the majority of term life policies offer the option to convert.

Registered investment FAQs

Who is eligible for a registered investment account?
Any individual who meets the account requirements can open a registered investment plan.
Why consider registered investments?
Registered accounts allow your money to grow while deferring taxes, and in some cases, you may qualify for tax credits or deductions, helping your savings grow more efficiently over time.
How do registered and non-registered investments differ?
Registered plans provide tax benefits, such as deferred growth or tax-free withdrawals, whereas non-registered accounts do not, and investment earnings are subject to taxation.
How do I pick the right registered plan?
Selecting a plan depends on your long-term objectives, risk appetite, and how accessible you need your funds to be. Guidance from a financial advisor can help identify the most suitable option for you.
Can I take money out of my registered investment?
Withdrawals are allowed but may have tax implications. For instance, TFSA and FHSA withdrawals are generally tax-free, while RRSP withdrawals are taxable. We can clarify the rules for your specific account type.

Non-registered investment FAQs

Are withdrawals taxed?
Yes, withdrawals are taxed on earnings, such as capital gains and dividends, though capital gains are taxed at a lower rate.
How does this differ from an RRSP?
Non-registered investments have no contribution limits and allow penalty-free withdrawals at any time, offering greater flexibility.
Can I invest in different asset types?
Yes, you can diversify your investments in non-registered accounts, including options like stocks, bonds, and mutual funds.
Who is this suitable for?
These accounts are ideal for those who’ve maxed out their RRSP/TFSA contributions and need flexible access to funds before retirement.
